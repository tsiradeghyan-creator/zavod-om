# Director Craft — Ռեժիսորական վարպետություն (FILM MODE)

World-class directing grammar, tuned to Ani's restraint-driven dramaturgy. Load when
planning any shoot or AI-rendered scene: the director decides WHAT the frame must make
the viewer feel; `cinematography.md` decides HOW the lens does it. Crew around the
director: `production-team.md`. The physical world: `art-department.md`.

## The Maghakian directing thesis

Her scripts hide emotion under behaviour — so the camera must TRUST the actor and the
beat. Default stance: **hold the shot, let the behaviour play, cut late.** Melodrama is
shot loud; Maghakian is shot quiet. When in doubt: wider, longer, stiller.

## Scene prep (before any shot is planned)

For every scene answer on paper:
1. **Objective** — what does each character want THIS scene (verb, not feeling)?
2. **Obstacle** — who/what blocks it?
3. **Tactic shifts** — where does the character change approach? (those are your cuts)
4. **The turn** — the one moment the scene exists for. Everything serves it.
5. **Subtext image** — what does the viewer see that the characters don't say?
   (her trademark: the hand twitching under the blanket while he says «թող ինձ հանգիստ»)

## Shot grammar (sizes = emotional jobs)

| Size | Job | Maghakian use |
|---|---|---|
| EWS/WS | world swallows character | war ruins, empty village, the ward |
| MS | neutral information | dialogue spine of daily series |
| MCU | intimacy begins | tactic shifts |
| CU | the truth leaks | the turn — held 2s longer than comfortable |
| ECU/insert | object = subtext | the «Տակնուվրա» objects: cigarette, anchor, line |

Rules that always hold: 180° line (cross it only AT a betrayal/reveal — crossing = the
world flips); eyeline height = power (look up at who controls the scene); push-in =
realization (slow, ≤10%); never zoom for emotion in drama registers.

## Shot grammar II — joins, POV, inserts, geometry

- **Transitions as meaning:** plan thread-joins at the directing stage — match cut
  (gesture/object rhymes across storylines) or pre-lap (next scene's sound under this
  scene's last CU). The editor can't invent these; shoot the rhyming frames.
- **POV three-shot unit:** look → what they see → reaction. The reaction IS the shot —
  never skip the third. Withhold the POV object = suspense; withhold the reaction = mystery.
- **Insert discipline:** one insert per scene earns meaning; three make a commercial.
  Shoot inserts last, lit to match the master.
- **Oner vs coverage:** oner only when real-time duration IS the meaning (waiting,
  dread); coverage when tactic shifts need cuts. Maghakian default: long takes WITHIN
  coverage — cut late, but have the cut.
- **Group geometry:** 3+ characters = triangles, never lines; the excluded character
  gets the depth axis. Re-triangulate at every tactic shift.
- **Screen-direction maps:** each intercut thread owns a direction (A moves L→R, B R→L);
  the convergence scene = the first frame they share an axis. Keep a per-episode
  direction map — the script supervisor logs it (`production-team.md`).

## Blocking patterns (hers)

- **The shield:** characters talk through objects/tasks (cooking, pottery, cleaning a
  wound) — block the activity FIRST, dialogue rides on it.
- **Distance = intimacy graph:** start the scene at maximum credible distance, let the
  text close it (or fail to). The blocking IS the relationship arc.
- **Doors & thresholds:** her episodes end on doors opening / someone collapsing — stage
  the cliffhanger object in frame from the start, ignored, so the payoff lands.
- **Intercut threads:** match energy direction across storylines (A ends rising → B
  opens falling) — pre-plan exit/entry frames so the editor can breathe.

## Working with actors (set language)

- Direct with **action verbs**, never emotions: not «ավելի տխուր», but «թաքցրու նրանից,
  որ ձեռքերդ դողում են»։
- **When stuck, ask, don't tell** (Weston question-form): «Ի՞նչ ես ուզում նրանից հիմա»։
  The verb the actor answers with becomes the adjustment.
- One adjustment per take. Praise specifically, adjust privately.
- **Rehearsal ladder:** block → run for camera marks → ONE full emotional run max (save
  it for the lens) → shoot. **Never burn the turn in rehearsal.**
- **Emotional continuity (out-of-order shooting):** keep an emotional-continuity map per
  character per scene number (1–10 intensity + what they know / don't know yet); check
  before every setup. The script supervisor owns the map (`production-team.md`).
- **Non-actors:** never give lines to memorize — give tasks and traps (a real activity +
  a secret instruction to one partner). **Children:** shoot their side first, shortest
  day, direct via games not verbs.
- **Video-village discipline:** only the director speaks to actors. Notes flow
  client → director → actor, never sideways. Monitor sound on headphones only.
- Her dialect registers (A1–A5) are sacred: cast for EAR first — an actor who can't
  speak Yerevan street can't be fixed in post (`production-team.md` casting).
- Rehearse the activity (the shield), not the lines — lines land when hands are busy.
- Daily-series economy: table-read the week on Monday, block-shoot by location,
  2 takes + safety. Save the third take for the turn only.

## Vertical / micro-drama directing (9:16, 60–100×1–2min)

- One scene = one episode = ONE turn + cliffhanger. No B-story inside an episode, and
  **no recap** — re-orientation happens via the first frame's composition, not dialogue.
- **Hook math:** conflict inside the first 5 seconds; ep-1's first 15s is the "explosion
  point" — consider dropping the season's most cinematic beat there as a built-in trailer.
- **Beat cadence:** a reversal or sharpened dilemma every 10–15 seconds — executed against
  the per-episode timestamp skeleton (hook 0–15s / friction 15–60s / spike 60–90s / button
  last 5–10s, `serial-structure.md`). Reconciled with her restraint: **hold the frame, not
  the beat** — stillness is allowed ONLY when the information inside it is escalating.
- **Button discipline:** the last shot cuts **on the question, not the answer** — frame the
  unresolved image (door ajar, hand frozen mid-reach) and hold it; never a resolved ending.
- **Script the reaction beat:** in 9:16 the processing IS the content — write what the eyes
  do, hand placement, the look-away as explicit action lines. In no-faces AI registers the
  same rule lands on the body: knuckles, shoulders dropping, breath, the half-turn — the
  reaction beat must be boarded (`storyboard.md` pose/body-blocking field), never implied.
- **Paywall (eps 8–12):** these get double prep time and the strongest cliffhanger of
  act one — that cliffhanger is the purchase. See `serial-structure.md` for placement.
- **Face-time ratio governs** (not wide-duration): 60–80% of shots are CU/MCU of faces
  and hands; one focal point per frame; friction must be filmable, not implied. Keep
  cutting wides to ≤3s — they read as pauses in vertical.
- Faces carry vertical: singles > two-shots; eyeline within 10° of lens.
- Block depth, not width: foreground object / midground face / background context.
- Hook discipline: the FIRST frame is a mid-action close-up (never an establishing shot);
  the last frame poses the question for the next episode (her cliffhanger DNA, 1:1).
- **Pacing economics:** vertical units shoot ~10 script pages/day; 2 takes + safety —
  the third take only on the turn and the paywall episode.

## Directing the AI pipeline (AI-rendered scenes are their own craft)

"Beat sheet + shot list" is the start, not the doctrine. Add:
- **Continuity bible per character/location:** wardrobe-by-silhouette, props, palette
  block — reused VERBATIM in every prompt. The reference-tag workflow (Seedance @Image /
  Kling Elements / Veo Ingredients) lives in `ai-pipeline.md`; the bible is the director's.
- **Performance via description:** no faces, so performance = body behaviour written as
  action verbs in the prompt («fingers tighten on the cup», never «she is sad»). Her
  action-line craft IS the AI performance direction — write it that way (`style-profile.md`).
- **Editing-driven directing:** AI takes are cheap, continuity is expensive — over-generate
  coverage of the SAME master frame, then direct in the edit. Lock entry/exit frames of
  each shot before generating the next (`cinematography.md` start/last-frame chaining).
- **Select/retake loop:** a reroll is undirected; a retake is directed. Rejection criteria
  = continuity break · accidental face · palette drift · wrong action verb. Name the fault,
  then regenerate against it.

## Director's pre-shoot checklist
- [ ] Every scene has objective/obstacle/turn on paper → feeds the board (`storyboard.md`).
- [ ] Shot list covers: master · OTS pair (or singles for 9:16) · the turn's CU · one
      subtext insert — each boarded with the full per-frame card (`storyboard.md`).
- [ ] The cliffhanger object is planted in an early frame.
- [ ] Register confirmed against `style-profile.md` (A1–A6/B/C) — tone drives coverage.
- [ ] No face appears in AI output (brand rule) unless licensed/owned footage.
