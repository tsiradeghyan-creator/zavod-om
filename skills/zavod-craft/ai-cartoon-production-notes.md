## Pipeline order (never skip, never reorder)

`idea → script → shot breakdown → character sheets → generation → montage → publish`

Generating pictures before the script exists is the classic beginner failure — you get pretty
frames and no story. Lock idea and script first.

### Idea → script (feeds `idea-lab.md`, `serial-structure.md`)

The course's scriptwriting prompt (full template in the vault) works as a repeatable brief:

1. **Inputs:** niche, target audience, desired audience outcome, core problem, main fear.
2. **Characters first:** propose ~5 recognizable characters tied to the niche (household objects,
   profession objects, body elements, products, tools, brand symbols). For each: name, look,
   character, why it's memorable, which topics it unlocks.
3. **Mini-serial of 3 Reels**, each continuing the previous:
   Reel 1 = attention via problem/funny situation · Reel 2 = escalate the conflict / common
   misconception · Reel 3 = solution + reason to subscribe.
4. **Script requirements:** hero speaks first-person; alive, conversational text; no officialese;
   no dull intros; light humor/self-irony; 30–45s; a new action/thought/visual accent every 5–7s.
5. **Per-episode structure:** strong hook → conflict/problem → development → unexpected insight →
   soft CTA → hook into the next episode.
6. After each script, write out separately: **visual scenes, character actions, character emotions,
   frame-generation ideas** (this is the shot breakdown that Seedance consumes).

## Montage / finish (feeds `post-production.md`, `sound-craft.md`, `motion-design.md`)

Good montage ≠ many effects. **Good montage = the viewer finishes the video.** Hold attention, don't
show off transitions. CapCut is enough for most jobs (works from phone).

- **Tempo:** if nothing happens for 5–7s, cut. Constantly ask "what is the viewer seeing right now?"
  If the shot already made its point, move on. Modern viewers expect fast cutting.
- **Plane changes:** alternate wide / medium / close-up / emotion (the shot breakdown already
  varies planes) — this keeps the reel alive.
- **Transitions:** plain cut, gentle fade, occasional morph. The simpler the transition, the more
  professional it reads. Logical continuity usually removes the need for effects entirely.
- **Voice = half the success:** watch volume, diction, emotion, no noise. A great cartoon is ruined
  by bad audio faster than by an imperfect picture.
- **Music sets mood** but must not drown the characters; CapCut's built-in library is enough for
  first projects (fun / atmospheric / adventurous / dramatic / kids).
- **Audio mix ladder:** **voice loudest → music below voice → SFX/ambience quieter still.** Never
  leave all tracks at equal volume.
- **Subtitles:** optional. Add them for no-sound viewing, complex/educational topics, heavy
  dialogue; most cartoons don't need them.
- **Pre-export 5-question check** — run before every render (works as an `editor-final-catch` gate):
  does the opening hook? is the story clear? is the pace alive? is the audio clean (voice > music)?
  do I want to know what happens next?

## Typical-mistakes QC checklist (feeds `qc-continuity-gate.md`, `continuity-qc-supervisor.md`)

Reviewer checklist — each item is a reject/needs-human signal for a cartoon deliverable:

1. **Plot too complex** → start with one hero, one situation, one conflict.
2. **Reel too long** → first projects 20–30s max; a finished short beats an unfinished epic.
3. **Character drifts between shots** → Character Sheet + reference every scene (`ai-cartoon-reel.md`).
4. **Style drifts between shots** → lock style/palette/atmosphere up front, hold it throughout.
5. **No script** → idea → script → breakdown → *then* generation. Never generate first.
6. **No dynamics** → a hero standing still loses viewers; every shot needs action/emotion/plane change.
7. **Bad voiceover** → viewers forgive an imperfect picture, never bad sound.
8. **Too many effects** → use effects only when they serve the story.
9. **Generation for generation's sake** → per shot ask "what job does this frame do?" No answer → cut it.
10. **Quitting too fast** → try 1) base gen 2) refine details 3) improve the prompt before changing the idea.
11. **Vague shots** → describe precisely; vague prompts let the model change the character/clothes/extras.
12. **No references** → always attach a reference when location/lighting changes or the hero recurs.

North star: don't chase a perfect cartoon — **finish** the project. Skill comes from **completed
works**, not from watching more lessons.

## Monetization & pricing (feeds `business-affairs.md`, `producer-craft.md`)

Young market, **no single price list** — specialists price differently, and that's normal. Five
models seen in the field:

| Model | How | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Per finished reel** | fixed price by length (≤30s / ≤1min / ≤2min) | beginners; client knows total up front |
| **Per second** | rate × duration (e.g. 15s ≈ 10k ₽) | commercial/ad, complex gen, many scenes/characters |
| **Per shot/scene** | price by scene count, not runtime | uneven projects (some shots 5 min, some hours) |
| **Per package** | e.g. 10 reels at once | efficient: characters/style/scripts reused, faster |
| **Retainer** | monthly fixed fee for ongoing cartoons/ads/social content | business clients; client often pays the AI subscriptions |

**Price drivers** (why two 30s reels cost differently): number of characters, number of scenes,
script complexity, dialogue, voiceover, montage, animation, urgency. Price the **volume of work**,
not just runtime.

**2026 market reference (RUB, orientation not a fixed price):**
- ≤30s reel: from ~6 000 ₽ · ≤1 min: ~10 000 ₽ · 15s commercial animation: ~10 000 ₽
- Business cartoon: ~10 000–25 000 ₽ · series (~10 reels): ~60 000–100 000 ₽+
- Ongoing content: from ~75 000 ₽/month (+ AI subscriptions billed separately)

Always adjust to your skill, speed, and project complexity. A per-reel or per-second quote should
still be justified by the work volume above.

## Client acquisition & commercial proposals (feeds `business-affairs.md`, `producer-craft.md`)

- **First-client mindset:** don't wait for the perfect portfolio or moment. The first client comes
  from outreach, not readiness — start showing work now.
- **Where to look:** own contacts, Telegram, VK. Exercise: list **≥10** people/companies you could
  pitch today.
- **Commercial proposal (КП) — two routes:**
  1. *Ready template:* warm intro → "I made a short cartoon specifically for your <business>" →
     what it does for them (lowers fear / attracts attention in socials / stands out from
     competitors / builds a recognizable image) → soft CTA to a 10–15 min call.
  2. *ChatGPT-generated:* a sales-specialist prompt that analyzes the company and writes a
     **personalized** proposal showing which business task the cartoon solves.
- **Rule:** there is no perfect template; never copy word-for-word. Show (a) what you help with,
  (b) what task the cartoon solves, (c) why it's useful for **this** client. More personalized →
  higher reply rate.
- **Client brief intake** (scopes + prices the job): company/contact/sphere/geography · goal
  (promotion / product ad / social content / awareness / kids / greeting / explainer / brand story)
  · main task + desired viewer action · audience (age, gender, interests; emotions to evoke) ·
  format (Reels/TikTok/Shorts/ad/mini-series/greeting/kids) · count · duration · platforms.
- Ready templates in owner vault `ԴԱՍ Ai/материалы/`: brief, character-card, mini-series script,
  commercial proposal.

## Niche & style self-selection (feeds `idea-lab.md`, `producer-craft.md`)

Positioning exercise before taking commissions: pick **3 favorite formats** (kids / animals /
family / greetings / ads / brand-characters / mini-series / explainers), **2–3 comfortable styles**
(the favorite style usually becomes the portfolio base), and **3 target niches** (e.g. dental,
beauty, cafés, online-schools, experts/bloggers, agencies, real-estate, vet/pet, family, wedding,
own socials). Then list the ≥10 first-client candidates above.
